Know the moment they're wheels up.

TailWatcher follows the planes and flights your household cares about on free, public ADS-B data, and pings everyone's phone through the ntfy app the moment one takes off. It also keeps an eye on the plane flying in for your next trip, so you hear it's running late before the gate agent tells you.

How it works

Your flight plan

Four waypoints from install to the first alert. Most people are done in about five minutes.

  1. 1
    WPT INSTL

    Install on StartOS

    Add TailWatcher to your Start9 server. It needs nothing else: no NTFY package, account or token.

  2. 2
    WPT WATCH

    Add a watcher

    Open the dashboard and tap + Add watcher for each person who wants alerts. A name is all it needs.

  3. 3
    WPT PHONE

    Connect their phone

    Connect phone makes a one-tap subscribe link for the free ntfy app. Text it to them, wherever they are.

  4. 4
    WPT WHEELS

    Wheels up

    Add a tail or flight number, from the dashboard or by texting it. The next takeoff lands on their lock screen.

Trips

Where's my plane?

Your airline app says "on time" right up until it doesn't. The plane you'll board is still in the air somewhere else, and that's the first place a delay shows. TailWatcher watches it for you.

  • No key needed. Text inbound UA1234 N38473 dep 4:30pm with the plane from your airline's "Where's my plane?" screen.
  • Or let it look things up. With a free AeroDataBox key, inbound UA1234 11/14 is enough. It finds your time and the assigned plane, and re-checks as the day gets closer.
  • Light on requests. A trip booked a month ahead uses about 20 of the free plan's 400 monthly requests.
  • Never changes things behind your back. If your flight is renumbered it suggests the new one; you confirm with change.
  1. 08:05
    🧳 UA1234: your plane is on an earlier flightN38473 is flying UAL412 DEN→ORD first, then on to IAD for your flight.

Install

Pre-flight checklist

TailWatcher is a StartOS 0.4 service. Download the latest package from GitHub and sideload it; a community registry listing is on its way.

  1. 1

    Download the package

    Get the .s9pk for your server from the latest release. Most servers are x86_64; ARM servers take aarch64. Each release lists SHA256 checksums.

    x86_64aarch64 (ARM)
  2. 2

    Sideload it

    In StartOS, use the Sideload option to upload the .s9pk. Updates work the same way: sideload the newer file.

    Prefer to build it yourself? You need Docker, Node.js and start-cli:

    # builds tailwatcher_x86_64.s9pk; make install sideloads it
git clone https://github.com/RogerUp-Labs/tailwatcher
cd tailwatcher
make
  3. 3

    Open the Web UI

    Start TailWatcher, open its Web UI, add a watcher for each person and tap Connect phone.

  4. 4

    Optional extras

    Paste a free AeroDataBox key in ⚙ Settings for automatic trip lookups, or switch alerts to your own NTFY with Set up NTFY for me.

FAQ

Questions from the cabin

Is it really free?

Yes. TailWatcher is MIT-licensed, the ADS-B feeds are free community projects, ntfy.sh is free, and AeroDataBox's Basic plan is free. There's nothing to subscribe to.

Which planes can it follow?

Anything broadcasting ADS-B that a volunteer receiver can hear: airliners, business jets, most private planes and many helicopters, worldwide. Coverage on the ground at small airports is thinner, so a takeoff there may arrive as "is airborne" a few minutes later.

Do I need my own ntfy server?

No. By default alerts go through the public ntfy.sh service, and each person's topic is a long random name. If you'd rather keep alerts on your own hardware, install NTFY on StartOS and tap Set up NTFY for me in Settings.

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes, with the free ntfy app from the App Store. Use ntfy.sh for instant iPhone alerts: a self-hosted NTFY can't use Apple's push service, so alerts may wait until the app is opened.

What about planes hidden from FlightAware?

The feeds TailWatcher uses are unfiltered, so planes in the FAA's LADD program still show up. Aircraft in the FAA's PIA privacy program broadcast a temporary address and can't be followed by tail number anywhere.

Why StartOS?

StartOS makes self-hosting a one-click affair, and a watcher that runs around the clock belongs on an always-on server, not a laptop. Your list of planes, trips and family members never leaves your house.

Who can see our alerts and trips?

Alerts reach only the phones subscribed to each person's topic. The dashboard is reachable on your home network and VPN, and you can turn on a password for it. Invite links expire and can be cancelled.

What is AeroDataBox for?

It's an optional flight-schedule API. With a key, inbound UA1234 11/14 finds your departure time and the assigned plane by itself. Without one, you tell TailWatcher which plane it is, which your airline app usually shows.

How often does it check?

Every 60 seconds by default, with every watched plane in a single batched request to stay well within the feeds' limits. If one feed says "too many requests", TailWatcher uses the other.

Can I run it without StartOS?

The watcher is plain Python 3.12 with no dependencies, so it can run anywhere, but the packaging, settings and setup flow are built for StartOS. Contributions for other platforms are welcome.
